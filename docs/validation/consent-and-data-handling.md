# Participant information, consent and data handling

*Read this to every participant before the session; give them a copy.*

**What this is.** A 45-minute session to find out whether a network configuration-drift tool is understandable and whether its risk ratings
match how network staff think. **We are testing the tool, not you.** Struggling with a task is useful information about the tool.

**What you will do.** Complete five short tasks on a *simulated* campus network (three virtual switches and routers in a laptop lab) while
saying aloud what you are thinking, answer a ten-question usability questionnaire, rate the seriousness of some configuration changes,
and answer a few questions.

**Nothing real is at risk.** The devices are simulators on the facilitator's computer. You will not touch any production system and you never
need to enter a real password: the facilitator signs you in with demo accounts.

**What we record.** Your role (for example "lab administrator"), years of networking experience, how each task went and how long it took,
questionnaire answers, ratings and notes of what you said. You are identified only as P1, P2, ... in all files and in the report. We do **not**
record your name, email, institution or voice/video unless you separately agree to screen recording; if you agree, recordings are kept only
until the notes are written and are then deleted.

**Where it goes.** Anonymous results go in the project report and repository (`docs/validation/data/`). Quotes are used only without
anything that could identify you.

**Your rights.** Taking part is voluntary. You can skip any task or question, stop at any time and ask for your data to be removed
afterwards, without giving a reason and without any consequence (including for any course assessment).
